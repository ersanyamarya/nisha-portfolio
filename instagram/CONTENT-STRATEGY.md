## 2. Voice and tone

- Warm and personal. Write in first person: "my setup", "back home". It should read as a home barista sharing a cup, not a brand selling one.
- Specific, not gushing. Give the real numbers: 92°C, 3s pre-infusion, 18g in, 38g out, 28s, grind 5.5. The numbers do the persuading.
- Emojis are optional. The profile uses one or two at the end of a line. Skip them when the line already works.
- End on a question. "Drop your go-to autumn roaster below" gets replies. Use it on most posts, not all.
- Tag the gear and the roaster. The @tags are part of the format, not decoration.
- English with a Berlin touch. Mention Berlin and local roasters when it is true.

## 4. Hook formulas (first 1–3 seconds of a Reel, or first line of a caption)

Specific beats generic. "Stop using trending audio if your product needs explanation" works better than "Stop doing this on Instagram". Patterns to borrow:

- Myth or mistake: "If your shot runs in 15 seconds, you're probably under-extracting."
- Result first: "Three changes took my cortado from sour to sweet."
- An opinion people can argue with: "Pre-infusion is overrated. I skip it on light roasts."
- Ask people to guess: "Guess the dose I used for this shot."
- Curiosity gap: "The one tweak that fixed my latte art."

Put motion or on-screen text in frame one, and deliver the payoff by second 3. After that, viewers swipe.

## 5. Content pillars

1. **Recipes and dial-ins** (highest performing). Espresso, cortados, filter. Always include the numbers.
2. **Latte art progression.** A weekly series, "fails to nails", different pours. Good Reel material.
3. **Gear and comparisons.** Grinder, machine, accessories. Attracts roaster and brand partnerships.
4. **Behind the scene at home.** The setup, the corner, the mess, the mistakes. Keeps the account personal.
5. **Seasonal and Berlin.** Autumn, roasters, cafés, local events. Ties the feed to the calendar.

Aim for 2–3 posts a week. Batch-shoot several at once in the same light and corner.

## 6. Trends and upcoming content (October 2026)

- **Autumn now, then winter.** Warm drinks, sweaters, golden light, books, window scenes. Autumn coffee content fits this account's theme (Endless Summer "autumn comfort" post already works).
- **Coffee Day and calendar hooks.** Tie posts to real dates. Upcoming: Halloween (31 Oct), then cold-weather brewing in November.
- **Save and share, not just likes.** One October 2026 source argues the posts that grow are the ones people save or send to a friend. Recipes are naturally saveable.
- **Keyword in caption and on-screen text.** Use one search phrase per post, such as "espresso dial-in" or "autumn coffee recipe", in the caption and spoken or on-screen text.
- **Caveat:** The October 2026 trend source is a single startup blog with some stray unrelated text. Treat its claims as one view. The other trend sources are from 2024 and 2025.

## 7. Growth research (what to do, what to avoid)

- Reels drive the most reach to people who don't follow yet. Keep them under 20 seconds, with a fast hook.
- Fix the profile before the content: clear bio, one niche ("home barista + UX designer in Berlin" works).
- Comment on niche accounts and reply to your commenters, especially in the first hour.
- Post fewer, better pieces. Three strong posts a week beats seven weak ones.
- Avoid: "like if you agree" bait, bought followers, 30-hashtag blocks, trending audio unrelated to coffee.
- Expect 8–12 weeks of consistent posting before the compounding signals show.
- Most of this is anecdotal. The sources conflict on specific numbers. Check Instagram's own guidance before relying on any exact figure.
